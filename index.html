I am an AI performance and systems engineer with more than a decade at the intersection of computer architecture, AI infrastructure, and high-performance computing. Most recently, in Intel's Datacenter Group, I characterized large language model and vision-language-action (VLA) robotics workloads end to end and turned that analysis into architecture direction: hands-on profiling on A100 and H200, roofline-based projection across edge accelerators, and the memory-bandwidth-versus-compute tradeoffs that decide real-time serving budgets. I led the adaptation of an LLM roofline performance simulator to multimodal workloads and was technical lead of a SPEC Cloud simulation effort advising AMD, Arm, and Google. Earlier, I led the Harvard–Intel graph-learning acceleration research that produced peer-reviewed papers, a US patent application, and optimizations upstreamed into PyTorch Geometric, and co-developed low-precision machine translation that won the 2020 WNGT efficiency task, with its INT8 kernels later adopted into SPEC CPU. My background is in physics and HPC: five years at EPCC supporting scientific applications on the UK national supercomputer, where I also organized an MSc course, co-developed a supercomputing MOOC, and led a UK-wide training program. The through-line is the same everywhere: balancing FLOP/s against TB/s at every layer of the memory hierarchy, and being clear about what is measured versus what is projected.

Selected Projects

Physical AI Workload Characterization (VLA and World Models)

Characterized VLA serving (π0.x) across model structure, attention behavior, transformer-diffusion balance, and BF16/INT8 execution; hands-on profiling on A100/H200 with roofline-based projection across Jetson-class edge, NPU, and dataflow accelerators. Isolated a memory-bandwidth-bound denoising loop that inverted compute-centric optimization priorities. Recommended lower-bit quantization and microscaling formats for real-time budgets; scoped world models as the next physical-AI workload wave.

LLM Roofline Performance Simulator (Multimodal Adaptation)

Led the adaptation of an existing LLM roofline performance simulator to vision and VLA workloads. The simulator supports arbitrary inference configurations: model architectures, parallelism strategies, attention variants, and disaggregated prefill/decode serving; its analysis feeds architecture direction.

SPEC Cloud Simulation and Performance Projection

Technical lead of a five-person effort reconciling measured benchmark behavior against modeled results and advising consortium participants including AMD, Arm, and Google.

Sparse Computational Infrastructure for GNNs Harvard × Intel

Established and led a cross-organization collaboration — around seven contributors across Intel and Harvard University (SEAS) — on graph-learning acceleration for Intel PiUMA. Owned inception, stakeholder management, software development and performance optimization — resulting in ISPASS 2023, a TASK Quarterly journal paper (2024) and a US patent application.

Low-Precision CPU Inference — Marian NMT in SPEC CPU

Part of the Intel–University of Edinburgh collaboration (K. Heafield) on CPU-efficient, low-precision (int8) Marian neural machine translation, which won the 2020 NGT efficiency task. The underlying int8 GEMM kernels were adopted into the SPEC CPU benchmark in 2026 — where Marian machine translation is the sole NLP workload.

Memory-Centric Inference Architectures: Silicon Photonics and Disaggregated Memory

Assessed silicon-photonics and rack-scale optical interconnect options for memory-centric inference, including commercial silicon-photonics interconnect solutions, comparing latency, throughput and cost across on- vs off-chip solutions; explored disaggregated compute/memory.

NextGenIO — Exascale Non-Volatile Memory

Built system-level workload characterization of ARCHER (UK national supercomputer) and researched data-aware, workflow-enabled HPC scheduling (SLURM-class) for Intel 3D XPoint non-volatile memory.
